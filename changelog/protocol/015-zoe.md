# Zoe

## Summary

Zoe introduces ZK proof version `%3` at height `119400`. Version `%3`
preserves the version `%2` AIR and proof layout, but binds the extra composition
polynomial to committed trace work at the post-commitment DEEP challenge and
domain-separates the mining digest from Fiat-Shamir output. It also binds the
new proof-version tag into post-activation block IDs and closes the height-zero
proof-version/checkpoint bypass. At the same forward-only cutover, Zoe re-pins
ASERT to the original Aletheia `2^291` target. The first Zoe block—and the
zero-drift ASERT baseline—therefore has approximately `536,870,911.375`
expected full proof attempts; subsequent targets evolve under ASERT.

## Grinding Feasibility

**Post-cutover work baseline.** Let `p = 2^64 - 2^32 + 1`, let `M = p^5 - 1`
be the largest Tip5 digest atom, and let `T_zoe = 2^291`. A uniformly
distributed full proof attempt passes the target with probability

```text
pi_zoe = (T_zoe + 1)/(M + 1) ~= 1/536,870,911.375.
```

The expected full-work cost is therefore about `536.9 million` attempts per
block, inside the `500–600 million` band; the stored fork-choice work is
`floor(M/(T_zoe + 1)) = 536,870,911`. After `q` independent full attempts, the
chance of at least one target hit is `1 - (1 - pi_zoe)^q`: approximately
`60.60%` at `500 million` attempts, `63.21%` at `536,870,912`, and `67.29%` at
`600 million`. These are full-proof baseline probabilities. They do not treat
cheap transcript or Merkle variants as full work; the analysis below separately
bounds those grinding paths.

The grinding path is economically relevant because it does not improve the chance
that one mining digest meets the target; it makes each additional digest much
cheaper. Let `tau` be the target fraction (the probability that one uniformly
distributed mining digest meets the PoW target), let `Q` be the number of
trace-reusing transcript variants. The Fiat-Shamir evaluation points used
below are in a field of size `p^3`. The formulas use the random-oracle and
uniform-index model; reducing a Tip5 field element modulo a power-of-two domain
introduces only negligible bias here. `epsilon_prox` covers both the query-point
link between the DEEP word and its commitments and the FRI folding soundness
error.

The probabilities that must not be conflated are:

| Strategy | Proof survival for one candidate | PoW-and-proof success after `Q` cheap candidates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `%0`-`%2` fixed-length coefficient mutation or permutation, followed by the object-`6` affine repair and a consistent late commitment | Approximately `1` whenever the repair slope is nonzero; the exceptional challenges are not a meaningful security barrier | `1 - (1 - tau)^Q`, approximately `Q*tau` while `Q*tau` is small |
| `%3` coefficient mutation with the already-committed trace held fixed | At most `epsilon_zoe <= (D - 1)/(p^3 - 2N) + 1/p^3 + epsilon_prox` for a polynomial with at most `D` coefficients | At most `min(1, Q*tau*epsilon_zoe)` by a union bound when one proof suffix is tried per candidate |
| One actual received-word leaf mutation in a trace root that is already in the PoW prefix | Under the uniform-index model, the eight top-level indices, selected distinctly modulo the final-codeword length, miss the changed position with probability `p_miss = 1 - 8/N`, where `N = 64H` and `H` is the padded maximum trace height | `1 - (1 - tau*p_miss)^Q`, assuming the challenge-dependent downstream prefix and suffix are rebuilt |

The DEEP challenge rejects two domains containing at most `2N` field elements,
which accounts for the denominator `p^3 - 2N` rather than an unconditioned
`p^3`. For the `D = 4096` instance considered here,
the direct post-commitment identity term is at most
`4095/(p^3 - 2N) < 2^-180`. A prover can instead repair a post-commitment
evaluation, but that makes the committed DEEP quotient a far, non-low-degree
word. In the simplest one-pole model, a false opening contributes `c/(X - y)`;
a root count limits agreement with a degree-`<H` word to about `H` of `N = 64H`
positions, giving the rate-based heuristic `(1/64)^8 = 2^-48` for eight ideal
samples. More general repairs introduce additional denominators and small
degree terms, so this is not a strict distance bound or, by itself, a proof of
soundness for the implemented sequential 8-ary FRI. `epsilon_prox` therefore
remains explicit. If a prover can grind `R` independently distributed proof
suffixes after finding one PoW candidate, PoW has already succeeded. Under the
independence model the conditional probability is at most
`1 - (1 - epsilon_zoe)^R`; without that assumption, the union bound
`min(1, R*epsilon_zoe)` still applies. Neither expression has an additional
`tau` factor.

The one-leaf probability is deliberately shown separately. For example, if
`H = 4096`, then `N = 262144` and the implemented residue-distinct sampler,
under the uniform-index model, misses one changed position with probability
`99.996948242%`, up to the negligible modulo-reduction bias. This does not forge
a computation when the miner retains the underlying honest codeword, but it
does make the changed root a cheap root-variation and trace-reuse nonce.
Rebuilding the challenge-dependent downstream proof objects is not free.

Reed-Solomon distance explains the `2^-48` estimate only when comparing two
valid degree-bounded codewords. Two distinct codewords of dimension `H` and
length `N = 64H` differ in at least `N - H + 1` positions, so the chance that
eight samples all land in their at-most-`H - 1` agreement positions is bounded
approximately by `(H/N)^8 = (1/64)^8 = 2^-48`. A one-leaf mutation is instead
an invalid received word at distance one from an honest codeword, so that
minimum-distance premise does not apply. Swapping unequal coefficients is just
another nonzero polynomial difference; swapping equal coefficients produces
no new encoding and therefore no mining candidate.

## Motivation

In versions `%0` through `%2`, proof object `5` is the extra composition
polynomial `P`, and proof object `6` contains trace evaluations at a challenge
sampled after `P`. Both objects are in the seven-object PoW prefix, while the
mega-extension commitment is object `7`.

That order leaves an affine grinding degree of freedom. A miner can change one
coefficient of `P`, derive the resulting challenge, and repair a suitable
coordinate in object `6` so the verifier's first equality still holds. The
expensive trace computation can be reused while producing additional PoW
candidates.

Moving an additional late trace-codeword Merkle root into the PoW prefix is not
an adequate repair. A miner can change one received-word leaf and update its
logarithmic Merkle path to obtain many cheap roots. With only a small number of
random openings, most attempts do not query the changed leaf. Reed-Solomon
minimum distance applies between valid codewords; it does not make a root
commitment to a nearby invalid received word expensive to vary.

## Technical Specification

### Proof version

The proof-version union gains `%3`. Version `%3` uses the same tables,
constraints, and serialized proof-object sequence as `%2`.

Consensus selects proof versions by block height:

```text
height < 6750             -> %0
6750 <= height < 12000    -> %1
12000 <= height < 119400  -> %2
height >= 119400          -> %3
```

This is a forward-only verifier change. A block is always checked against the
proof version selected by its own height, so a current node continues to apply
the historical `%0`, `%1`, and `%2` rules to already-mined blocks.

The height-selected proof-version predicate is shared by normal and genesis
validation. Height zero therefore requires `%0`; it cannot be retagged to a
later proof version even though historical block IDs do not bind the tag.

### ASERT re-anchor

The `%zk` ASERT schedule gains a newest-first row whose first child height is
the existing Zoe cutover, `119400`. Its anchor is therefore the parent at
height `119399`, and its target is the existing Aletheia consensus constant
`2^291`. Heights `112500` through `119399` retain the prior three-million-
proofs-per-second anchor, which corresponds to approximately `450 million`
attempts per ideal 150-second block. The Zoe row restores the established
target rather than introducing another calibration constant.

The dynamic anchor timestamp is the accepted parent branch's median-of-11
timestamp. The first child reads the accepted anchor directly; accepting that
child and each subsequent descendant carries the timestamp forward in the
existing puzzle-keyed, block-digest-keyed cache. Miner target construction and
validator target checking call the same scheduled ASERT routine, so the height
and target cannot diverge between production and validation.

Dynamic re-pin lookup is O(1). The fallback that recursively followed parent
blocks to rediscover an anchor timestamp is removed. At the first child the
anchor is the direct parent and its median timestamp is already an O(1) state
lookup; after that, the branch cache carries the same value forward. A missing
cache entry is treated as incomplete consensus state and fails closed. Side
forks remain isolated because cache entries are keyed by both puzzle type and
the accepted branch's block digest.

### Post-commitment extra-composition check

Let `P(X)` be proof object `5`. The existing verifier samples the DEEP point
`y` after absorbing the mega-extension and composition commitments (objects `7`
and `8`) and reads committed trace evaluations in object `9`.

For `%3`, the verifier additionally requires:

```text
C_extra(object9, y) = P(y)
```

The later DEEP quotient, FRI, and Merkle-opening checks bind object `9` to the
trace commitments. No new proof object is added, and an honest prover performs
no additional commitment or Merkle work.

For `%3`, object `5` must also be a structurally valid canonical polynomial
(`P = bpcan(P)`) with at most `D` coefficients, where the processed extra-
constraint degree bound is `D - 1`. Canonical form removes trailing-zero
encodings as transcript entropy; the upper bound prevents an out-of-range term
from bypassing the advertised degree. The bound is not an exact-length rule,
because an honest random linear combination can have a lower degree through
leading-coefficient cancellation. Consensus has separately required canonical
PoW polynomials since height `112500`; the `%3` verifier makes both invariants
explicit at the proof boundary as well.

Every `%3` polynomial/vector payload must have a logical length matching its
backing atom. Merkle openings additionally have exact semantic widths: base,
extension, and mega-extension leaves match their respective trace widths;
composition leaves match the statically derived number of composition pieces;
and each FRI opening contains exactly one folding coset. The composition-root
piece count must equal the maximum constraint degree. These checks prevent a
miner from changing a hashed length header or appending verifier-ignored values
to obtain a new root or Fiat-Shamir challenge while presenting the same
codeword values. Native decoding rejects a claimed polynomial length before any
unsafe slice is formed unless the backing atom has the historical word shape;
for legacy versions this means it contains at least the declared payload words,
including historically ignored suffixes. The `%3` raw-proof boundary
additionally requires the exact size and terminal marker.
That version gate leaves historical `%0` through `%2` marker acceptance
unchanged while making Hoon and native `%3` decoding agree.

### Per-table trace degree binding

The DEEP polynomial also contains an independently weighted batch

```text
R(X) = sum rho[t,j] * X^(H - h[t]) * T[t,j](X)
```

where `H` is the maximum table height, `h[t]` is table `t`'s declared height,
and `T[t,j]` ranges over every committed base, extension, and mega-extension
trace column. The cubic-extension weights `rho[t,j]` are sampled after the
trace roots and claimed DEEP evaluations are fixed.

An honest column has degree strictly less than `h[t]`, so every term in `R`
has degree strictly less than `H`. Adding a multiple of the table zerofier
`X^h - 1` preserves every row value but creates a degree-`H` term in `R`, which
the existing strict FRI bound rejects except with the probability of a random
leading-coefficient cancellation.

### Mining-domain separation

All versions continue to mine over proof objects `0` through `6`, which already
include the base and extension trace roots at objects `2` and `3`. No additional
late root, especially the mega-extension or composition root at objects `7`
and `8`, is moved into the PoW prefix. For `%0` through `%2`, the PoW digest is
unchanged. For `%3`, let `prefix_digest` be the legacy Tip5 digest of objects
`0` through `6`; the mining digest is:

```text
Tip5([leaf+%zkpow-v3 hash+prefix_digest])
```

This prevents the mining digest from being reused as an unlabeled
Fiat-Shamir squeeze while retaining the inexpensive, pre-Merkle mining loop.

### Block identity

The historical full-proof digest hashes proof objects but not the proof-version
tag or proof-stream bookkeeping fields. Version `%3` blocks instead commit a
tagged digest of all verifier-visible proof fields in the block ID. Its domain
tag is:

```text
Tip5([
  leaf+%zkblk-v3,
  leaf+%3,
  hash+legacy_full_proof_digest,
  list+(hashes),
  leaf+read_index
])
```

Versions `%0` through `%2` retain their exact historical block-proof digest.
This prevents a `%3` block from being retagged as `%2`, or having its proof
cache/index metadata mutated, under the same block ID. It changes no
already-mined block ID or proof Fiat-Shamir state.

### Genesis checkpoint

When the configured genesis seal identifies realnet, genesis intake requires
the height-zero digest in the checkpoint map. Custom networks retain their
existing behavior and skip realnet checkpoints. This preserves the canonical
already-mined genesis while rejecting alternate legacy proof encodings or
proof-version retags under the realnet seal.

  and zero-drift baseline have approximately `536.9 million` expected full

- **Target date**: approximately `2026-08-06`

## Migration

### Requirements

- Software version: the Zoe reference implementation or newer.

### Configuration

None.

### Data Migration

None. Historical blocks and proofs are not rewritten. During load, every node
with a recognized network identity audits the canonical tip and (once crossed)
the stored height-`119400` activation page; realnet additionally audits every
pinned checkpoint page. The audit checks the height-selected proof version,
page digest, canonical proof-stream envelope, and expected checkpoint ID where
one is pinned. Durable kernel state advances once, from `%10` to `%11`. The
single `%11` marker covers both Zoe's proof-version and ASERT semantics. If a
`%10` state's accepted canonical tip has reached `119400`, the migration resets
consensus state and resynchronizes rather than
reinterpreting its targets or cached timestamp. The decision reads the heaviest
page directly in O(1); it does not scan ancestry. Existing boot-time proof audit
and orphan cleanup reject or delete noncanonical post-cutover work before the
node can process another event.

A late `%9` upgrade whose accepted tip crossed the earlier dynamic anchor with
no timestamp cache is reset for the same reason. A nonempty cache is not enough:
the migration requires the canonical tip's own branch-keyed entry, so a stale
side-fork-only entry cannot mask an unusable canonical cache. It also requires
the canonical tip's normal median-timestamp entry, which both validation and
candidate construction consume. Pending pages at or above `119400` are rejected
through the normal index-repair path. A pending header's
monotone observed-height marker does not by itself reset an otherwise valid
pre-cutover chain. The persisted mining candidate and accumulator are discarded
and immediately rebuilt through the normal candidate constructor under the new
target, before startup can emit mining work. Consensus resets preserve custom
network constants as well as the existing boot and mining configuration.

On subsequent `%11` loads, only noncanonical post-boundary entries are decoded
and audited before the same load pipeline removes orphaned entries. Canonical
pages are identified from the height index without cueing their proofs, so
recurring restart cost does not grow with the post-Zoe chain; the boundary page
and current tip are still checked explicitly.
This rejects a pre-Zoe node state that has already accepted `%2` at the `%3`
boundary, including on custom networks. A pending page also repeats the cheap
version gate when its final transaction arrives, so persisting it across the
upgrade cannot bypass activation.

The load audit is a migration consistency check for state produced by the
node itself, not full proof replay. Operators must fully revalidate any
untrusted or externally edited state import.

The ASERT reset requires no new durable-state shape. Kernel state already
contains the per-puzzle, per-branch dynamic-anchor timestamp cache. At the
exact cutover, the height-`119399` anchor is the direct parent and its median
timestamp is available by an O(1) state lookup. Accepting the height-`119400`
child carries that value forward for subsequent blocks, without scanning older
chain history. Operators must deploy before constructing or accepting the
height-`119400` child; an incomplete post-cutover cache fails closed.

A born node with no accepted chain is left untouched while it waits for
genesis, but only when its chain-bearing stores are empty. Ambiguous or partial
chain state is reset rather than preserved in a permanently wedged state. A
reset retains the BTC anchor, genesis seal, initialization state, and mining
configuration so genesis intake can safely resume.

### Steps

1. Deploy the upgraded verifier and miner before height `119400`.
2. Confirm candidate blocks below the boundary still request `%2` proofs.
3. Confirm candidate blocks at and above the boundary request `%3` proofs.

### Rollback

Do not roll back to a `%2`-only node after activation; it will diverge from the
network's proof-version rule.

## Backward Compatibility

The proof noun gains a new discriminant, so software predating Zoe cannot
decode or validate post-activation `%3` blocks. Upgraded software remains fully
compatible with historical blocks because the stronger equation and mining
domain apply only to `%3` proofs.

## Security Considerations

The second equality is evaluated at a cubic-extension-field challenge sampled
after both relevant roots are committed. For any fixed inconsistent `P`, the
difference from the trace-derived extra composition is a nonzero polynomial;
the probability that it vanishes at `y` is bounded by its degree divided by the
extension-field size.

That identity argument requires the committed trace to be in each table's
claimed Reed-Solomon degree class. The normalized trace batch enforces those
per-table degree bounds. In particular, a miner cannot add `c(X^h - 1)` to a
trace column: although that mutation is invisible on the `h` table rows and its
ordinary DEEP quotient fits the old global bound, its normalized term has
degree `H` and fails v3 FRI.

The late roots are intentionally not added to PoW. Cheap mutations of a
committed received word can vary a Merkle root, but each such root only samples
another `y`; it does not let the miner choose `P` after seeing `y`.

The base and extension roots were already part of the historical PoW prefix.
Zoe removes representation-only root nonces: a changed length header or ignored
leaf suffix is rejected even if its leaf is queried. It does not eliminate the
distinct, pre-existing sparse-Merkle strategy of changing one actual
received-word value and hoping the random openings miss that position. As
quantified in Grinding Feasibility, the direct miss probability for one changed
position is `1 - 8/N`, not `2^-48`. This is the remaining Merkle-twiddling skip
point rather than a deterministic coefficient/evaluation nonce. Zoe does not
add the late mega-extension or composition roots to PoW and thereby does not
create more instances of that root nonce.

The approximately `2^-48` rate-based sampling heuristic applies directly to
the simplest one-pole false-opening model; additional denominators perturb the
elementary agreement bound. It is not substituted for a complete soundness
analysis of the implemented DEEP link and sequential FRI. Combined with the
cubic-extension identities and the independent trace-degree batch, a
conservative per-fixed-suffix summary therefore keeps that term symbolic:

```text
epsilon_zoe <= (D - 1)/(p^3 - 2N) + 1/p^3 + epsilon_prox
```

Grinding `R` distinct post-PoW transcript suffixes scales this bound by at most
approximately `R`. Zoe removes the cheap deterministic coefficient repair; it
does not claim more than the existing roughly 48-to-50-bit FRI parameter target
or claim that sparse queries make a one-symbol received-word mutation unlikely
to be missed.

## Operational Impact

Honest proof size and Merkle work are unchanged. Verification adds one
extra-composition evaluation at the existing DEEP point plus one evaluation of
`P`, the per-query trace-degree normalization batch, and canonical proof-shape
checks. The `%3` mining digest adds one fixed tagged Tip5 hash after the legacy
seven-object prefix digest.

The ASERT row adds no proof-verifier work and no chain-history traversal. It
changes the selected ASERT anchor baseline at height `119400`; scheduled target
selection remains constant-time, and subsequent targets evolve normally.

### Verifier benchmark

The release Roswell harness prepares authentic `%2` and `%3` proofs for the
same length-`64` puzzle in separate, unmeasured events. It then alternates the
two verifier versions in counterbalanced, equal `%2`-then-`%3` and
`%3`-then-`%2` pairs on a fresh ephemeral kernel, with four warmups per
version. Both proofs are resident before timing, so no proof serialization or
per-arm proof ingress/copy is timed. The timed boundary is the NockApp verifier
poke, including common dispatch, cause/effect copying, and state cleanup;
kernel boot, proof generation, command construction, effect decoding, PMA, and
event-log I/O are excluded.

```text
RUST_LOG=error target/release/roswell --new --ephemeral \
  bench-verifier --runs 16 --warmups 4 --puzzle-length 64
```

On an Apple M4 Max with 16 cores and macOS 26.5, using the release profile and
Rust `1.96.0-nightly`, the primary run measured:

| Proof version | Median | Mean |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `%2` | `340.85 ms` | `340.85 ms` |
| `%3` | `518.87 ms` | `517.32 ms` |

The median of the per-pair Zoe deltas was `+178.874 ms`, or `+52.15%`; the mean
per-pair delta was `+176.472 ms`, or `+51.78%`. (A paired median need not equal
the difference between the separately reported marginal medians.) An
independent 16-sample run measured `+52.68%` paired median and `+50.68%` paired
mean overhead. This is a steady-state verifier-event comparison, not a full
node block-validation or throughput benchmark. For this length-`64` workload
on the tested system, deployments should budget for approximately 52% more
proof-verification latency until the additional composition evaluation and
trace-degree normalization are optimized without weakening their
post-commitment binding.

## Testing and Validation

- `%0`, `%1`, and `%2` public proof fixtures continue to verify unchanged.
- A genuine `%3` proof generated under the normalized DEEP rule verifies in
  both the Hoon and native Rust verifiers.
- A `%2` transcript merely relabeled as `%3` is rejected because its DEEP
  commitment does not include the v3 degree-binding batch.
- `%3` encodes and decodes as the noun atom `3`.
- Height `119399` selects `%2`; heights `119400` and `119401` select `%3`.
- The `%zk` ASERT schedule retains the 112500 anchor at height `119399`,
  selects the Aletheia `2^291` anchor at heights `119400` and `119401`, and
  computes exactly that target for the first Zoe block.
- The height-`119400` child propagates its branch-local anchor timestamp: its
  child target still computes with retained block bodies removed, while the
  same state with the timestamp cache removed fails closed even if the parent
  chain is available to walk.
- Height zero selects `%0`, and an identical-object `%0` proof retagged `%1`
  is rejected even though the legacy block digest does not bind that tag.
- Realnet accepts the pinned height-zero digest and rejects an alternate;
  fakenet genesis seals continue to bypass the realnet checkpoint map.
- The stored-page load audit accepts a height-zero `%0` page and rejects its
  identical-object `%1` retag, nonempty hash cache, or nonzero read index.
- Load rejects missing pinned checkpoint pages, a preloaded `%2` activation
  page on realnet or a custom network, a stored post-cutover `%2` side fork,
  a mixed `%3/%2/%3` canonical suffix from a pre-`%11` state, and ambiguous
  partial-genesis state; it preserves a genuinely empty born state that is
  waiting for genesis.
- The state-`%11` migration resets a state-`%10` canonical chain that has
  already reached `119400`, resets a cache-starved late state-`%9` upgrade past
  `112500`, rejects a side-fork-only cache or missing canonical median timestamp,
  preserves a pre-cutover state with a canonical-tip dynamic-anchor cache and a
  real pending-height marker, drops its stale Zoe pending page,
  and rebuilds its mining candidate at the new target.
- A pending height-`119400` `%2` page is rejected when transaction-complete
  validation resumes after the upgrade.
- Genesis intake cleanly rejects a wrong proof version, a missing production
  proof, and the wrong page variant; test-only PoW skipping does not skip the
  version gate when a proof is present.
- `%2` PoW remains the legacy prefix digest; `%3` PoW differs by its explicit
  domain hash.
- `%2` and `%3` proofs with identical objects retain the same raw transcript
  digest but produce different block-proof digests and block IDs.
- Retagging a `%3` block as `%2` while retaining its digest fails the page
  digest check.
- A table-zerofier shift reaches degree `H` in the normalized trace batch and
  is rejected by the strict `< H` FRI bound.
- `%3` rejects a trailing-zero encoding of object `5` and a canonical object
  `5` whose degree exceeds the processed extra-constraint bound.
- `%3` rejects malformed array length headers, oversized trace/composition
  openings, a wrong composition-piece count, and non-coset-sized FRI openings;
  the Hoon and native decoders/verifiers agree on these shapes.
- Proof-stream push/pull preserves `%3` through the FRI sub-verifier. A
  noncanonical terminal marker remains decodable under the historical `%2`
  rule but is rejected at both Hoon and native `%3` proof boundaries.
- `%3` rejects non-based Merkle sibling limbs before any release hash jet can
  normalize them modulo the field.
- Adversarial review must test coefficient/evaluation repair, sparse Merkle-root
  grinding, transcript challenge bias, and downgrade/activation behavior.
